# Hooks

The hook is the first 1–2 seconds of a video, the first slide of a carousel or the first line of a
caption. Its job: make the right person stop. Always offer 2–3 options; the owner picks.

## Patterns

| Pattern | Example (fictional) |
|---|---|
| Mistake | "You're storing your bread wrong." |
| Question | "Why does homemade sourdough come out flat?" |
| Number | "3 things I check before every wedding cake." |
| Contrast | "Same flour, two loaves. Only one was rested overnight." |
| Promise (true) | "How to keep a cake fresh for 3 days." |
| Story | "A customer cried when she saw this cake. Here's why." (only if it happened and she agreed) |
| Behind the scenes | "4am at the bakery." |
| Direct callout | "If you sell at markets, watch this." |

## Rules

- Specific beats clever. Name the topic in the hook.
- Spoken hook and on-screen text say the same thing (most people watch muted).
- The video must deliver what the hook promises, early.
- No clickbait the video does not pay off, no fake shock, no invented numbers.
- No health, money or result promises (see `safety.md`).
- Match the owner's voice; avoid stock phrases ("You won't believe…", "This changed my life").
